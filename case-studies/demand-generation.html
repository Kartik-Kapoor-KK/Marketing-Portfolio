Making A Webinar Work Harder.

Turning a single webinar initiative into a coordinated demand generation motion across promotion, registration, engagement and follow-up.

01

The Challenge

A Webinar Is Not A Campaign. Yet.

The event itself is only one moment. The marketing opportunity exists before, during and after it.

The challenge was to make the webinar generate more attention and registrations by coordinating the surrounding marketing activity rather than treating promotion as a single announcement.

That meant thinking about the audience, the message, the channels and the follow-up as parts of one connected journey.

02 / The Campaign Architecture

One Event. Four Moments.

The campaign was approached as a journey rather than a single promotional activity.

01 ATTRACT

Create Attention

Use relevant messaging and content to give the audience a reason to stop and pay attention.

→
02 CONVERT

Earn The Registration

Make the value of attending clear enough that the audience is willing to exchange attention for registration.

→
03 ENGAGE

Deliver The Experience

Connect the campaign promise with an event experience that reinforces the value of the topic.

→
04 EXTEND

Keep The Conversation Moving

Treat the event as a starting point for further engagement rather than the end of the campaign.

03 / The Marketing Mix

Every Channel Had A Job.

Social Create Awareness

Build visibility and make the topic relevant to the audience.

Email Drive Action

Move interested audiences toward registration and participation.

Content Build Context

Give the audience enough context to understand why the conversation matters.

Follow-Up Extend The Value

Continue the conversation after the live event.

04 / The Result

WEBINAR REGISTRATIONS
37%

More People Showed Up.

The campaign approach contributed to a 37% increase in webinar registrations.

The result reinforced the value of treating demand generation as a connected system, where each touchpoint has a specific role in moving the audience forward.

05 / The Takeaway

The Event Was The Destination.

The campaign was the journey.

Good demand generation does not ask a single channel to do everything. It gives every touchpoint a job and connects those jobs into a coherent movement from awareness to action.

That way of thinking continues to influence how I approach campaigns today.
